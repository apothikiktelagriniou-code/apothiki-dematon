2. Luggage, parcel, small parcel, courier

The words look alike but mean different things, and different rules apply to each.

Accompanied luggage
A suitcase or item travelling with the passenger.
Unaccompanied luggage
Luggage travelling without a passenger. The 2023 regulation allows this service on regular intercity lines.
Unaccompanied parcel or small parcel
An item handed in as a separate shipment, with a receipt and recipient details. It does not automatically mean full-value insurance or home delivery.
A personal item left on the bus
Not the same as a parcel formally handed to the parcel service. The regulation treats it separately.
Courier service
A different regime, licensed by EETT, with added services such as tracking, proof of delivery and agreed times.

3. Law 4974/2022: contracts, tenders and agents

  • Award (art. 8): routes are awarded by open tender or, exceptionally, directly, with an exclusive right or an exclusive right plus compensation.
  • Duration (art. 20): ten years, extendable by up to five more.
  • Parcels (art. 12): unaccompanied small parcels are recognised, handled through facilities and authorised agents and carried in the luggage hold.
  • Tenders (art. 15): each tender also sets the terms for unaccompanied parcels.
  • Transition (art. 31): existing operators continue until awards are completed. In November 2026 tenders were still under way.

4. Decision 362897/2023: prohibitions and duties

What is prohibited (art. 4)

  • Dangerous, flammable, sharp, poisonous, dirty or foul-smelling substances, and generally anything that threatens passengers' safety.
  • Animals do not travel as parcels. They travel only with their owner, in the passenger area, under conditions.
  • Unaccompanied parcels are not allowed on regular international lines outside the EU.

The list we apply is in our Terms of service.

What the carrier must do (art. 5)

Every carrier on regular intercity lines must have an organised parcel reception service, and its staff must handle the receipt and delivery of luggage and unaccompanied small parcels responsibly.

5. Compensation (Annex I of Decision 362897/2023)

CaseWhat applies
Loss of luggage on intercity, international and special linesUp to €1,200 per item if its value is proven, otherwise €200.
Loss of unaccompanied luggage on regular intercity linesMinimum compensation €40.
Loss of or damage to luggage in an accidentUp to €1,200 per item.

"Minimum" does not mean "maximum". The Annex refers to "unaccompanied luggage" and does not separately define every kind of commercial small parcel. In a specific case, the category, proof of hand-over, contents, value and cause of damage are examined.

6. Complaints and deadlines (art. 6)

  1. Complaint to the carrier

    In writing, within 3 months. You are updated within 1 month and get a final answer within 3 months of receipt.

  2. Appeal to the Region

    If not satisfied, within 1 month of the answer, to the Directorate of Transport and Communications. Update within 1 month, result within 2 months.

  3. Fines (art. 8)

    Breaches carry fines of €150–3,000 paid to the State. These are not compensation to the sender.

7. KTEL and EETT: when it is a postal service

Under EETT's licensing rules (Decision 1101/3/2024, art. 8), simply carrying an item is a postal service only when combined with another stage, such as collection from the sender, sorting or delivery to the recipient. A registration with EETT is then required.

So neither "every KTEL parcel is a courier service" nor "KTEL can never fall under EETT" holds as a general rule. It depends on what exactly is offered. Station-to-station carriage differs from collection and delivery at home.

8. Company terms and the Civil Code

Each KTEL publishes its own terms, such as a maximum cover, a deadline for claims or a maximum value of contents. These differ between companies and are not national law.

Article 332 of the Greek Civil Code makes void any prior agreement that excludes or limits liability for intent or gross negligence. A printed limitation does not override mandatory law. Whether it applies depends on the facts of each case.

The parcel is the responsibility of the company that issues the receipt. For shipments with a connection, ask which company handles each leg.

9. If a parcel is lost or damaged

  • Immediately: call and send a written report the same day.
  • Keep: the receipt, photos, invoices or proof of purchase, messages, and the outer packaging if there is damage.
  • Record: contents, value, dimensions, packaging, hand-over and arrival times, every contact.
  • Ask for: a logged report, a check of the route and a written answer on compensation.
Template for a first written report

Subject: Report of loss/damage of an unaccompanied parcel and request for investigation

On [date/time] I handed a parcel for [destination] to [agency] of [company], with receipt [number]. The contents were [description], worth [amount], as shown in the attachments.

On [date/time] [loss / non-delivery / damage] was found. Please log this report, check the route and inform me in writing of the outcome and the company's position on compensation.

Attached: receipt, photos and proof of value.
