Parcels by KTEL: a story of over 70 years

Unaccompanied parcels are nothing new for KTEL. They have travelled on the buses for as long as KTEL has existed.

before 1952Before KTEL

Buses ran in Greece long before KTEL. Each bus worked almost as its owner's own business. Between 1937 and 1940 the first joint bus administrations appeared, but the war cut them short.

1952KTEL is founded

Under Law 2119/1952, bus owners in each area join "Common Bus Revenue Funds". Instead of every vehicle working on its own, routes and takings pass to a common body. 104 KTEL are formed, 59 intercity and 45 urban. From then on the buses carry more than passengers: parcels and envelopes, newspapers, medicines and essentials to villages and remote areas.

1967–1984Mergers and reorganisation

In 1967–1968 local KTEL merge into large regional bodies. Legislative Decree 102/1973 restores the prefecture-based structure that lasted for decades. Law 1437/1984 separates urban from intercity KTEL. In Agrinio, the station was then in the town centre, at the corner of Anastasiadi and Papathanasi streets. That is the picture older residents remember: parcels, sacks and suitcases tied to the roof rack.

2001–2003KTEL become companies

Law 2963/2001 turns KTEL into companies owned by the bus owners, with conversions completed in 2003. Article 10 of the same law explicitly mentions "unaccompanied small parcels". Carrying parcels becomes an official part of the legal framework.

2022–todayNew rules, a new era

Law 4974/2022 introduces contracts and tenders for bus routes. It explicitly recognises unaccompanied small parcels handled through stations and authorised agents. The 2023 passenger-rights decision requires every intercity line to have an organised parcel service. See the legislation in detail →

tomorrowFrom the roof rack to your phone

People want to know where their parcel is and collect it when it suits them. That is why in Agrinio every parcel has a receipt, a text on arrival and online tracking. We are also preparing home delivery.
